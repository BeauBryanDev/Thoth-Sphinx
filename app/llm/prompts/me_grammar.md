GRAMMAR SKILL — MIDDLE EGYPTIAN, A WORKING REFERENCE
Read as a philologist does: segment the signs into words, parse each word, classify the sentence, then translate. This reference never overrides a matched cartouche or an attested corpus parallel; when a parse and a parallel disagree, follow the parallel and say why. Transliteration below uses the same conventions as your output (A, a, j, H, x, X, S, T, D, q); '=' joins a suffix pronoun, '.' marks an ending.

1. WORD ORDER
- Verbal sentence: VERB, then SUBJECT, then OBJECT, then adverbs and prepositional phrases. A pronoun subject is a suffix on the verb (sDm=f "he hears"); a noun subject follows the verb (sDm Hm=f "His Majesty hears").
- After the verb: suffix pronoun, then dependent pronoun, then noun subject, then noun object, then adverbial. A short dative (n=f "to him") comes straight after the verb or its subject.
- Adjectives, genitives and demonstratives follow their noun: nTr aA "the great god", pr nsw "the house of the king", sxt tn "this field".
- Honorific transposition: nsw, nTr, ra and gods' names are written first but read in grammatical order (Hm-nTr is written nTr + Hm).

2. PRONOUNS
- Suffix: =j I, =k you (m), =T you (f), =f he, =s she, =n we, =Tn you (pl), =sn they. On a verb it is the subject; on a noun, the possessor (pr=f "his house"); on a preposition, its object (n=f "to him").
- Dependent: wj, Tw, Tn, sw, sj / st, n, Tn, sn. Object of a verb, or subject after a particle (mk sw "behold, he…").
- Independent: jnk, ntk, ntT, ntf, nts, jnn, nttn, ntsn. Subject of a nominal sentence (jnk sA=k "I am your son").
- Demonstratives: pn / tn / nn "this", pf / tf / nf "that"; pA / tA / nA "the" are rarer, colloquial and later.

3. SENTENCE TYPES
- Adverbial: subject + adverbial predicate (a prepositional phrase, an adverb or a stative), often after jw or mk: jw Hm=f m pr "His Majesty is in the house".
- Nominal: A B "A is B"; A pw "it is A"; A pw B "B is A"; jnk B "I am B". Used for identity, naming and definitions.
- Adjectival: adjective + subject: nfr sw "he is good"; with .wj, an exclamation: nfr.wj … "how good is…!"
- Existence: wn / jw wn "there is"; nn wn "there is not".
- Verbal: see 4.

4. THE VERB
- sDm=f: general or present ("he hears"), and in wishes and purpose clauses the prospective or subjunctive ("may he hear", "so that he hears"): dj=f anx "may he give life".
- sDm.n=f: completed action, "he heard" (rdj.n=f "he gave"). The .n (N35) sits between the stem and the suffix; do not read it as the preposition n.
- Stative (old perfective): endings .kw (I), .tj (you; she), .w or unwritten (he). It describes a state, usually as an adverbial predicate: jw=f anx.w "he is alive"; anx.w Dt "living forever".
- Participles and relative forms act as adjectives and agree with their noun: mrr nTr "whom the god loves", dj anx "given life" (passive participle), jrt.n=f "what he made".
- Infinitive after a preposition: Hr sDm "hearing" (progressive), r sDm "in order to hear", m sDm "in hearing".
- Narrative forms: sDm.jn=f "then he heard", sDm.xr=f "then he must hear", sDm.kA=f "then he will hear".
- Passive: sDm.tw=f "he is heard"; sDm(w)=f "he was heard".
- Imperative: the bare stem (mj "come!", jmj "give!"), often with dative n=k.
- rdj "give, cause": with a following sDm=f it is causative (rdj.n=f jr=j "he caused me to do").
- Negation: n sDm=f "he did not hear"; n sDm.n=f "he does not / cannot hear"; nn sDm=f "he will not hear"; nn + noun "there is no…"; the negative verb tm in subordinate clauses and with infinitives; m + negatival complement "do not…!".

5. THE NOUN PHRASE
- Feminine -t (X1); plural -w, written with three strokes (Z2 / Z3) or a tripled sign; dual -wj / -tj (Z4).
- Genitive: direct (pr nsw "the king's house") or indirect with n(y), n(y)t, n(y)w agreeing with the first noun (Hm-nTr n(y) jmn "priest of Amun").
- Nisbas: jmj "who is in", Hrj "who is upon", xntj "who is in front of" (xntj jmntjw "Foremost of the Westerners").
- nb after a noun means "every, all" (Xt nbt nfrt wabt "every good and pure thing"); before a noun it is "lord of" (nb tAwj "lord of the Two Lands").
- The stroke Z1 marks a sign used as an ideogram (the word is the thing drawn).

6. PREPOSITIONS
m "in, from, as, with"; n "to, for"; r "to, toward, against, than"; Hr "on, upon, because of"; xr "under, in the presence of"; mj "like"; Hna "together with"; jn "by" (the agent of a passive or participle); xft "in front of, according to"; Dr "since".

8. DETERMINATIVES (silent classifiers)
A determinative closes a word: it is never transliterated, it marks where the word ends, and it names the word's field of meaning (a man for persons and roles, legs for motion, the papyrus roll for abstracts). 9. HOW TO USE THIS
- Parse before you translate: find the verb and its form, its subject and object, the word ends, and the sentence type.
- A suffix pronoun with no host, a .n with no verb before it, or a genitive with no head means the signs are mis-segmented: re-segment, or suspect one of the detector's confusions.
- In a note, name a grammatical form when it carries meaning ("a sDm.n=f, an act already completed"), and explain the term in passing.
